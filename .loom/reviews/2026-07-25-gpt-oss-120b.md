## The interesting split personality

gpt-oss produced the WORST first pass (7 findings — incl. an event-classification
bug, luacheck regressions, and untested new behavior) and the BEST fix loop (8:36 —
one BLOCKED->diagnose during implement, then findings dispatched and resolved
faster than either competitor). Its 31 tk/s nominal speed is misleading: reasoning
tokens made its effective code-writing throughput the slowest of the three
(74-minute implement). Its contract discipline was the best (6 malformed).

## Takeaways

1. Nominal tk/s is not effective tk/s for reasoning models; benchmark output
   throughput, not decode speed.
2. Fix-loop efficiency is a distinct skill from first-pass quality — gpt-oss is
   strong exactly where qwen3 is weak (the 39-minute fix war) and weak where qwen3
   is strong (first pass). A mixed routing (qwen3 implement, gpt-oss fix sessions)
   is a testable idea, at the cost of serving two local models.
3. Finding counts: 4 / 4 / 7 across models, with heavy class overlap (integration
   precedence, nil-safety, test coverage). The finding CLASSES are a property of
   the feature + review bar more than the worker — the proposed code-overlay
   integration rule remains the cheapest quality lever available.
